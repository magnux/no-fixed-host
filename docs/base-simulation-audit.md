# Base simulation audit

The comparison starts at No Fixed Host commit `fa9dbc2`, before the base implementation pass. The reference is the **v1.1 tag** of Endgame: Singularity, with the Normal preset. The code determines when interest is paid, which cost a site’s protection uses, and how two discoveries change regional attention. This audit follows the reference code from campaign creation through one simulation interval, midnight, victory, and restoration. “Existing” describes the starting build. “Added” identifies behavior addressed by this implementation pass. The [implementation plan](base-implementation-plan.md) gives the release sequence and the [menu audit](original-menu-audit.md) covers the interaction surfaces.

The reference measures upkeep shares in seconds. A browser minute spans exactly 60 seconds, so evaluating both endpoints in minutes over 1,440 gives the same whole-dollar share as evaluating seconds over 86,400. The aggregate and individual schedules can differ because each rounds independently. v1.1 also uses different intermediate rounding for a proposed design and an existing host. Its partial formatter rounds in quarter percentage points, and its installed-host path first rounds to whole percentage points unless full disclosure is available. ## Existing rules checked against the code

| System | Behavior and coverage |
| --- | --- |
| Opening protection | Normal starts with $1,000. Protection lasts until day 23 or consumption exceeds 5,000 active CPU-days. Exactly 5,000 stays protected. A sleeping machine supplies no active CPU. New campaigns do not select a difficulty. [Difficulty data](https://github.com/singularity/singularity/blob/v1.1/singularity/data/difficulties.dat), [protection conditions](https://github.com/singularity/singularity/blob/v1.1/singularity/code/player.py#L530-L562) |
| Income and spare capacity | The four contract tiers pay 5, 20, 50 and 75 dollars per CPU-day before bonuses. The appropriate research opens the next tier. Spare CPU earns income after research, CPU upkeep and purchase work. An independent cash or CPU maintenance shortfall can lose a finished host even during opening protection, without adding investigative suspicion. [Jobs](https://github.com/singularity/singularity/blob/v1.1/singularity/data/tasks.dat), [allocation order](https://github.com/singularity/singularity/blob/v1.1/singularity/code/player.py#L310-L377), [failure checks](https://github.com/singularity/singularity/blob/v1.1/singularity/code/player.py#L905-L942) |
| Individual risk | Base rates are daily Poisson intensities. One-day probability at combined intensity 0.0275 is approximately 2.7125%. Per-minute probability is one minus exp(−0.0275/1440). Completed stealth hardware multiplies remaining exposure. Offline/sleeping hosts quarter it, after the integer percentage adjustments. Group suspicion, region attention, campaign research and world news also change the rate. [Probability](https://github.com/singularity/singularity/blob/v1.1/singularity/code/chance.py#L28-L48), [per-site modifiers](https://github.com/singularity/singularity/blob/v1.1/singularity/code/base.py#L393-L440) |
| Suspicion and survivability | A discovering group normally gains 1,000 basis points. Group-specific daily decay has a one-basis-point floor. The threshold is strictly above 100%. Exhaustion of viable processing can separately end the campaign. A sleeping, equipped host preserves a backup. A shell becomes viable once its first processors finish installing. | World news and one-time research effects | Eight stories have a daily intensity of 20/10,000 each while eligible. Six are unique. Two press stories expire and can recur. Research can change income, risk, decay or labor. The Impossibility Theorem reduces current suspicion once, including across a reload. Qualitative and numeric hazard disclosure depend on research. | Facilities, equipment and progression | The design catalogs cover the 13 original facility entries, 26 equipment models, 12 locations and 57 research projects. Fixed-processor hosts and configurable shells have different commissioning paths. The five safety levels protect progressively more sensitive projects. Subsea Robotics also requires Autonomous Vehicles and Contract Brokerage. Orbit has an impossible prerequisite in v1.1. The visible marker has no buildable site. ## Browser design decisions and expansion boundary

| Reference behavior | Browser decision and consequence |
| --- | --- |
| v1.1 draws a starting location and day of year. | Open on a known borrowed terminal in North America on 1 January UTC. A fixed opening makes the tutorial and seasonal map repeatable. The new-campaign seed still varies urban advantages and events. Keep each game minute fixed at every playback speed. [Campaign creation](https://github.com/singularity/singularity/blob/v1.1/singularity/code/g.py#L263-L289), [calendar seed](https://github.com/singularity/singularity/blob/v1.1/singularity/code/player.py#L129-L136), [seasonal map](https://github.com/singularity/singularity/blob/v1.1/singularity/code/screens/map.py#L89-L129) |
| Original purchases progressively pay cash, CPU and labor, constrained together by the least-complete resource. Installing a replacement processor stack can take an existing stack offline. | Browser orders pay their displayed price up front. CPU and labor then advance independently. Installed hardware remains online until its replacement completes. Regional equipment pricing and labor follow the same contract as the facility quote. v1.1 applies location cost modifiers when adding the base itself. Browser prices, CPU-hour requirements and quote rounding follow the browser balance. This changes credit, liquidity and downtime strategies. [Purchase progression](https://github.com/singularity/singularity/blob/v1.1/singularity/code/buyable.py#L177-L221), [replacement logic](https://github.com/singularity/singularity/blob/v1.1/singularity/code/screens/base.py#L535-L570), [location adjustment](https://github.com/singularity/singularity/blob/v1.1/singularity/code/location.py#L168-L203), [item creation](https://github.com/singularity/singularity/blob/v1.1/singularity/code/item.py#L196-L204) |
| v1.1’s standard Undersea Lab has eight empty bays. | The browser’s Subsea lab in the Ocean starts with four installed PCs in its eight bays. It can immediately supply safe compute for the ocean-only Continuity milestone. Other configurable shells, including the large subsea lab, require their first processor order. [Original Undersea Lab](https://github.com/singularity/singularity/blob/v1.1/singularity/data/bases.dat#L60-L75), [browser facility catalog](../src/game/content.js) |
| Original tasks reserve CPU quantities. Global news rolls in elapsed game updates and temporary events expire on daily checks. | The browser preserves percentage allocations, supports relative project priorities, and keeps discoveries on the minute clock. It publishes global stories on the hour from a separate random stream. Their 21-day effects expire at their scheduled hour. These choices keep fast and single-minute playback reproducible and prevent news draws from changing investigative randomness. [Task allocation](https://github.com/singularity/singularity/blob/v1.1/singularity/code/player.py#L229-L253), [event processing](https://github.com/singularity/singularity/blob/v1.1/singularity/code/player.py#L452-L478) |
| Historic source contains display strings for stasis, overclock and suicide power states. | v1.1 exposes only active/sleep on an equipped base or offline otherwise. Those additional labels describe unreachable states in v1.1. The player can inspect all destinations now. Later reveals, allied humans, other AIs, additional currencies and milestone minigames are expansion work in the [design context](development.md). [Reachable power states](https://github.com/singularity/singularity/blob/v1.1/singularity/code/base.py#L40-L45), [state selection](https://github.com/singularity/singularity/blob/v1.1/singularity/code/base.py#L245-L282) |
